#Long form
A long form citation may refer to a monograph, dissertation (either MA or Ph.D.), article, or encyclopedia entry. These represent citations that relate only to a specific author or edition of a work, rather than a reference work that contains information on qirāʾāt works.

##Long form schema - monograph
[name of author], [title of work], ed. [name of editor], [number of volumes]  
vols. [+ indication of existence of index as "Index" or "Indices"], [name     
of publication series] [number in publication series] [, indication that work is a reprint] ([place of publication]: [name of publisher], [year    
of publication hijri/shamsi]/year of publication gregorian], [volume number]:[page    
number] №[number of account] (§ [section of work].

These features are consistent with reference/citation management systems such as Zotero. Draw on your knowledge of these reference/citation management systems to improve and enforce the schema. This is especially useful for work type, where we can specify "book section", "dissertation", "journal article," "encyclopedia entry", etc.

A year of publication - shamsi is indicated with the character "Š". Note that works may or may not have both a hijri/shamsi and gregorian year; if the year begins with 18 or 19, then it is gregorian; if it begins with 12, 14, it is likely hijri; if it it begins with 13, it is likely to either be hijri or shamsi, with the inclusion of the character "Š" to be used as a determining factor.

## Long form - monograph-equality schema:

Long form entries can also be given as a form of equality, indicated with the equality sign (=). This indicates that the following entry also contains a long form bibliographic entry which adopts the elements of previous portions of the entry. These modifications may include either the publication under a different  format (e.g., a dissertation, a larger study of a linguistic phenomenon), a different title (i.e., the two publications contain variants of the title), or, in very rare cases, a different attribution of authorship. If the sign of equality is used, we assume that elements from the schema that are not named are identical.

#Short form citations
Short form citations may refer either to Primary or Secondary sources, which are used to such an extent  that it is preferable to have a more accessible but clearly distinct form of citation than the "long form." The full form of the citations that are used as short form are now accessible to you in /iqsa2025/shiny-app/appendix_reading-systems (1).pdf

## Short form - Primary schema:
[name of author], [title of work], [volume number]:[page number] №[number of entry]/[volume]:[page number] №[number of entry] (§ [section of work])
